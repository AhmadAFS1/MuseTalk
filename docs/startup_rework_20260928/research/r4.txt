CROSS-GPU HISTORY, PROVEN. Every per-GPU number from Mar to Jul 2026 was measured on the SD-VAE decoder path (trt_stagewise FP16 or 5-stage INT8). There the VAE took 55-76% of each GPU batch. WebRTC 20fps aggregate plateaus (fps = streams/avg_interval) and strict-smooth 20fps stream counts:
- V100-32GB sm70, FP16 stagewise: 4,8,16 gave about 48-52 fps; 24-only gave 48.5-56.8 fps. bs20 and bs32 OOM. Smooth 1-2 streams. INT8 and TRT UNet were never tested.
- RTX3090 sm86: FP16 8,16 gave 45-47 fps at 24.1 GB. INT8 bs8 gave 56-58 fps at 8.3 GB. INT8 8,16 gave 62-65 fps at 17.9 GB. Adding TRT UNet split8 gave 70.8-72.3 fps. The exact16 UNet gave 65.6/70.6/69.0 fps at C4/6/8 (17.6 GB). Dual 8+16 UNet engines OOM at 22.7 GB. Backend GPU-timed rate at bs8 is 94-97 fps. Smooth 3-4 streams.
- RTX4090 sm89, FP16 only: bs8 gave 55 fps at 10.6 GB. 8,12 gave 61-62 fps at 20.7 GB. 8,16 OOM in live VAE decode at C6. INT8 and TRT UNet were never tested.
- RTX5000Ada 32GB: FP16 60-61 fps, then INT8 70-72 fps at 17.7 GB (PyTorch UNet). Smooth 3 streams.
- RTX6000Ada 48GB: FP16 73-81 fps, then INT8 108-111 fps, then INT8+split8 112-114 fps at 19.6 GB. UNet bs8 is 15.0 ms here vs 26.4 ms on the 3090. 8,16,32 failed to create a TRT execution context. Smooth 4 streams.
- No A100 or H100 data exists anywhere in the repo.
Two rules held on every card: aggregate fps ≈ batch/avg_gpu_batch, and extra VRAM or bigger buckets never raised the good-stream ceiling.

SUPERSEDED BY TAESD (4070S sm89 12GB, 220W cap, only arch measured):
- Model path at bs8: UNet 25.9 ms + TAESD 12.4 ms gives 209 fps at 2.09 GB (+0.31 GB per 8 frames).
- Sustained GPU path (TRT bs8 UNet + compiled TAESD) held 260 fps over 200 s, at 24.2 + 6.3 ms per bs8.
- The live 10-stream wall run (contaminated) gave 136-145 fresh fps. The structural single-process ceiling is 174-203 fps.
- The UNet is now about 79% of GPU time. So capacity ≈ 8000/(UNet_bs8_ms + TAESD_bs8_ms + ~1) × live efficiency, where efficiency is 0.53-0.85 on record.
- PROJECTED ceilings from the measured UNet latencies: about 220 fps on a 3090 and 350+ on an RTX 6000 Ada. The live path, not the GPU, would bind first.
- INT8 VAE, VAE late-block work, the VRAM-class bucket table and SD-VAE warmup budgets are obsolete.

WHAT BINDS ON A NEW BOX, in order:
1. An arch-matched, validated UNet TRT artifact. The engines are not portable, the S3 bundle is rtx3090/sm86, and the meta files record no compute capability. A build needs captures, 184-401 s, about 11 GB host RAM, 2.2 GB disk and +1.9 GB VRAM.
2. Host RAM. Loading the UNet .ts costs a transient +8.4 GB, so boot needs ≥12 GB MemAvailable. Each session adds 0.43 GB, and the startup high-water is 10.5 GB.
3. The GPU power cap. A 150 W 3090 ran 3.7x slower.
4. The single-process serving path: syncs, the blocking handoff, idle decode, and the GIL on the chin path.
5. The encoder. aiortc 1.14 hardcodes libx264 at its default (medium) preset, about 7.85 cores at 300 fps. Native VP8 on one thread needs about 1.85 cores for 15 streams. NVENC is capped at 12 sessions on GeForce.

CPU: keep workers at 8/8/8. 12/12/12 on a 64-core host did not help, and aggressive thread caps dropped GPU util to 37%. The chin path uses about 10.6 logical CPUs at 300 fps.

ARCH:
- FP8 is dead on sm89 with TRT 10.3.
- torch.compile of the UNet failed the quality gate.
- Compiled TAESD is proven only on 56 SMs, where inductor skipped its GEMM autotune. GPUs with ≥68 SMs will run the autotune, with unmeasured compile time.
- The pinned torch 2.5.1+cu121 / TRT 10.3 stack cannot target Blackwell (projected).

- Engine portability is unchecked. vast_onstart.sh restores the rtx3090 sm86 bundle by default (required), unet_trt_meta.json carries no GPU/compute-capability/TRT-version fingerprint, and the selector accepts any meta with validation.passed=true. - Doc/code disagreement on the UNet profile. vast_onstart.sh:34 defaults MUSETALK_TRT_PROFILE_PREFER=split8, while docs/webrtc_generation_optimization_results_2026-07-03.md says 'use exact16-only for new 3090 servers' and select_unet_trt_profile.py defaults to exact16. Logs claim NVENC (the launcher prints 'h264_nvenc(default)'), which hides about 7.85 cores of x264 at 300 fps.
- Profiling syncs are on by default in production: HLS_GPU_STAGE_SYNC_TIMING=True (hls_gpu_scheduler.py:230) and MUSETALK_VAE_DECODE_TIMING/_SYNC=True (musetalk/models/vae.py:29-30). The wall log confirms gpu_stage_sync_timing=True.

- STARTUP_TIMEOUT_SECONDS=600 (vast_server_ctl.sh:19) is too short for a first-boot UNet TRT build (torch_tensorrt 401 s without timing cache) plus model load. Historic SD-VAE warmups needed 2400-3600 s timeouts.
- There is no startup check for a GPU power cap (a 150 W 3090 ran 3.7x slower) or for the cuInit=999 wedge where nvidia-smi works but CUDA does not. vast_onstart.sh:238 only checks torch.cuda.is_available(), and concurrent_gpu_manager falls back silently to 24 GB.

The TAESD wall run (launcher bypassed) logged aggressive_fill_max_active_jobs=4, versus launcher 999. - Worker sizing uses os.cpu_count() (hls_gpu_scheduler.py:181,196), which ignores cgroup quota, and floors compose/encode at 6 threads even on small containers.
- The 'split8' and 'int8' names in MUSETALK_TRT_PROFILE_NAME and the S3 bundle describe the SD-VAE era. Under TAESD, the 10 INT8 VAE plans, calibration/vae_decoder and modelopt are dead weight in install and restore.
