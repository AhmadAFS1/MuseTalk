This established the requirement to judge source motion and composited mouth separately. The low-VRAM native graph uses one portrait at guide indices `0` and `-1`; those guides alone did **not** guarantee decoded cross-clip equality.

## Cheek distortion: measured clues, limits, and next test

The most recent request was to explain cheek deformation during slight upward-looking motion. In [api_avatar.py](../scripts/api_avatar.py), preparation detects a **frame-specific** face rectangle, adds lower margin, crops that rectangle to 256 × 256, and encodes its latent. At composition, MuseTalk resizes the generated face back into that frame's rectangle and blends it through a frame-specific face mask. [blending.py](../musetalk/utils/blending.py) suppresses the upper part of that mask but includes the lower cheeks and jaw. Consequently a source pose that moves the head changes both the apparent face geometry and the exact region where synthesized pixels replace the source.

The preliminary [Latina measurements](../../experiments/musetalk_sdvae_vs_taesd_new_avatars_20260925/latina/cheek-source-output-mask-measurements.json) show this clearly. Talking frame 0 has a 215 × 320 face box with mask starting at image y=299 and 20,820 strongly blended pixels. At upward-motion frame 60 the box is 213 × 374 (about **17% taller**) with the mask starting at y=266 and 28,494 strongly blended pixels (about **37% more**). Width hardly changes. Frame 80 returns close to baseline. The [Japanese measurements](../../experiments/musetalk_sdvae_vs_taesd_new_avatars_20260925/japanese/cheek-source-output-mask-measurements.json) show a similar temporary face-box and mask expansion. This is a correlation with the suspected pose, not yet a causal isolation of source deformation, face detection, mask placement, VAE, or generated lower-face shape.

The [Latina](../../experiments/musetalk_sdvae_vs_taesd_new_avatars_20260925/latina/cheek-source-output-mask-sheet.jpg) and [Japanese](../../experiments/musetalk_sdvae_vs_taesd_new_avatars_20260925/japanese/cheek-source-output-mask-sheet.jpg) inspection sheets put raw source, SD-VAE composite, TAESD composite, and crop/mask overlay side by side at sampled frames. The reproducible sampler is [inspect_cheeks.py](../../experiments/musetalk_sdvae_vs_taesd_new_avatars_20260925/inspect_cheeks.py). A first inspection of Latina frame 40 showed both decoder outputs behaving similarly while the composite changed the lower face. The whole sequences have **not** yet had a documented normal-speed, frame-matched cheek review; avoid saying MuseTalk alone caused the distortion. Because both decoders use the same prepared coordinates/mask and look alike, swapping SD-VAE for TAESD is not a supported cheek fix.

Next controlled work: identify the exact distorted timestamps in the received or side-by-side clips; compare their raw LTX frames to both composites at identical frame indices; plot source facial landmarks, prepared box/mask area, and cheek contour over the entire sequence; then test one variable at a time. A source rerender that suppresses upward chin motion is reasonable if the raw LTX motion already creates the problem, but the accepted prompt pack already asks for a level chin, so text alone is no guarantee. If raw frames remain sound and only composites deform, test a more stable crop/mask or constrained lower-face blend on the **same** prepared/video input, preserving lipsync and checking side effects across all poses. Produce normal-speed A/B video before changing the approved source recipe or runtime.
